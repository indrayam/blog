About Me

I was born in 1972 in Hindmotor, a quiet industrial suburb of Calcutta, the youngest of three brothers. I grew up among equations, engines, and expectations, in the familiar rhythm of an engineering family. In time that turned into something larger: a lifelong fascination with systems, ideas, and what might be possible.

The path ran through Hartley’s High School, Jain Vidyalaya, and then the gauntlet of India’s engineering entrance exams. They earned me a seat in Mechanical Engineering at the Regional Engineering College in Kurukshetra, now NIT Kurukshetra. In 1995 I crossed an ocean to Arizona State University with little more than curiosity and ambition. I came for one master’s degree and left with two: Industrial Engineering, with a minor in Information Systems, and Computer Science. But the real education happened elsewhere: in late-night terminals, in elegant abstractions, in the quiet satisfaction of understanding how things fit together.

In the summer of 1996, an SGI workstation running IRIX offered me a glimpse into a different world. Everyone else admired the graphics. I fell in love with the blinking cursor. The terminal became more than a tool; it became my way of making sense of complexity. Decades later, the machines have changed. The scale has grown from a single workstation to fleets of cloud systems spanning continents, and tools like Claude and Codex have turned the once-humble command line into a launchpad for intelligent automation. The fascination hasn’t changed at all.

I still like to stay close to the metal, at the bit-and-byte level of the software factory floor. I think of my work as building bridges: between business and technology, between theory and practice, between the questions people ask and the answers they’re trying to find. Whether it’s AI, distributed systems, mathematics, investing, or software architecture, my instinct is the same: look beneath the surface until the hidden structure shows itself.

But the story isn’t only about technology.

It’s about long walks measured in miles rather than minutes, and shallow but wide-ranging reading. It’s about music that resonates, and unstructured time spent doing absolutely nothing with my wife, Shannon, who has shared the adventure through every chapter. It’s about being a pain for our two kids: Emma, who is studying physics, and Zachariah, who is drawn to manufacturing and mechatronics, where mechanical engineering, electronics, and software meet. It’s about a family spread across generations and continents, from my brothers to my father in Noida. And it’s about our Cairn terrier, whom I alternate between loving and hating, and who reminds us all that joy rarely comes in proportion to size.

The years have taught me that knowledge is valuable, but understanding is rarer. That certainty is overrated, while curiosity compounds like interest. That optionality is one of life’s great gifts. And that the most interesting questions rarely have immediate answers.

So I keep learning. One more book. One more command. One more model. One more idea worth exploring.

Still following that blinking cursor into the unknown.
